# Before and after

Read this when the strip pass leaves a sentence with nothing in it, or when a fix
risks inventing content the draft never carried. Each pair keeps the draft's
facts exactly. Where a tell was hiding the absence of a fact, the repair is to
flag the gap, not to fill it.

## Copula avoidance and significance inflation

> The portal serves as a pivotal moment in our tooling strategy, acting as the
> single entry point for all internal requests.

The 2 verbs are doing no work and `pivotal moment` rates something the draft
never measured.

> The portal is the single entry point for internal requests.

## The -ing clause hung off the end

> We moved the schema into one file, streamlining the developer experience and
> ensuring alignment across teams.

Both clauses gesture at outcomes the draft does not state. Cut them.

> We moved the schema into one file.

If the writer meant a specific outcome, that is a **Flags** line asking what it
was, never a guess.

## Significance inflation hiding a missing number

> The migration significantly improved performance and the team was thrilled.

`significantly` is standing in for a delta the draft doesn't have.

> The migration improved performance.

Then flag it: the draft claims an improvement but names no measurement, so the
number has to come from the writer.

## Restating the reader's position

> Your understanding that offers only apply to active users is correct. To
> confirm, offers do only apply to active users.

> Offers apply to active users only.

Or quote and reply, which keeps the thread readable:

> `> do offers apply to inactive users too?`
>
> No, active only.

## Rhetorical question and answer pairs

> Want to know what changed? We rewrote the validator.

> We rewrote the validator.

Genuine stacked questions are the opposite move and stay as they are:

> what does this do? does it look for the key, or a specific port?

## X, not Y antithesis

> This is not a ranking, not a leaderboard, and not a competition. It's a way to
> see who's shipping.

> It shows who's shipping.

## False ranges

> The changes touch everything from tooling to culture.

`tooling` and `culture` sit on no shared scale, so the range means nothing.

> The changes touch the build tooling and the review process.

Only if the draft named both. If it didn't, cut the sentence and flag it.

## Inline-header lists that restate themselves

> **Performance:** Performance improved after the migration.
> **Reliability:** Reliability is now better.

The label and the payload say the same thing twice.

> Performance and reliability both improved after the migration.

A label earns its place when the payload adds something:

> **Schema in TypeScript.** Tables live in one file.

## Passive voice with a hidden actor

> Queries are validated before execution and errors are surfaced to the caller.

> The compiler validates queries before execution and returns errors to the
> caller.

Passive stays where the actor genuinely doesn't matter:

> The row was deleted in the 3am job.

## Synonym cycling

> The portal handles auth. The platform also logs each request, and the tool
> retries failures.

Three names, one thing, and the reader re-reads to check.

> The portal handles auth. It also logs each request and retries failures.

## Hedging the ask instead of the diagnosis

> I think it might be worth possibly considering whether we could perhaps update
> the doc at some point.

The hedges have migrated onto the instruction.

> I think the doc is out of date. Update it.

## The warm closing sentence

> Let me know if you have any questions. Happy to help, and hope this helps!
> See you out there.

Delete all of it. End on the ask, or end on nothing.

## Over-correction, the opposite failure

> We shipped the API. We tested the API. We documented the API.

Three matched clauses read as generated even though every word is plain. Let the
shape go uneven, and keep the one repeated term.

> We shipped the API, tested it, and wrote the docs. Docs are thin in places.
